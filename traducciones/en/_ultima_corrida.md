## Pieza: fri_07_escalera_exvotos

### titulo

**ES:** Los exvotos: milagros pintados en lámina

**EN:** Ex-votos: Miracles Painted on Tin

### frase_gancho

**ES:** Frida reunió más de cuatrocientos cincuenta exvotos, casi todos dedicados a la Virgen del Rosario de Talpa.

**EN:** Frida gathered more than four hundred fifty ex-votos, almost all of them dedicated to the Virgin of the Rosary of Talpa.

### puente_narrativo

**ES:** Termina de subir la escalera de piedra y entra al estudio, el cuarto más luminoso de la casa.

**EN:** Finish climbing the stone staircase and step into the studio, the brightest room in the house.

### guion_corto

**ES:** Un exvoto es una pintura pequeña, casi siempre sobre lámina, que alguien manda hacer para dar gracias a un santo o a una virgen. Se pinta el peligro que la persona superó, y abajo se escribe lo que pasó. Frida coleccionó cientos de ellos. El museo habla de más de cuatrocientos cincuenta, y la mayoría están dedicados a la Virgen del Rosario de Talpa. Ella misma dibujó su accidente con ese estilo. Mientras subes, mira cómo se cuentan las desgracias sin miedo, con la sangre y el hospital a la vista. Ahí está una de las raíces de su pintura. Los verás en las salas y en la escalera.

**EN:** An ex-voto is a small painting, almost always on tin, that someone commissions to give thanks to a saint or a virgin. It shows the danger the person survived, and below it a few lines tell what happened. Frida collected hundreds of them. The museum speaks of more than four hundred fifty, and most are dedicated to the Virgin of the Rosary of Talpa. She even drew her own accident in this style. As you climb, notice how misfortunes are told without fear, with the blood and the hospital in plain view. Here lies one of the roots of her painting. You will see them in the rooms and on the staircase.

### guion_largo

**ES:** Antes de subir al estudio, vale la pena detenerse en esos cuadros pequeños que cuelgan en los muros. Son exvotos. Es una palabra que viene del latín y significa por una promesa. Una enfermedad, un accidente, una caída, un incendio. /  / Casi todos siguen una misma fórmula. En la parte de arriba aparece el santo o la virgen. Los verás en las salas y en los muros de la escalera. /  / Frida no solo los coleccionó. También hizo un dibujo de su accidente con ese lenguaje, según cuenta el museo. **EN:** Before going up to the studio, it is worth pausing at those small pictures hanging on the walls. They are ex-votos. The word comes from Latin and means by a promise. In Mexico, people have them painted to give thanks to a saint, to Christ or to a virgin for having saved them from danger. An illness, an accident, a fall, a fire. /  / Almost all of them follow the same formula. At the top appears the saint or the virgin. In the center the scene of danger is painted, with the person in peril or already saved. Below is a handwritten text that tells what happened and gives thanks. Often they were painted on commission by a neighborhood artist. /  / Frida collected many of these paintings. The museum speaks of more than four hundred fifty pieces, which make up one of the most complete collections in the country. According to the museum itself, most are dedicated to the Virgin of the Rosary of Talpa, a much-venerated devotion in Mexico. Frida painted her wounded body with the same frankness. /  / The poet André Breton, leader of surrealism, visited Mexico in nineteen thirty-eight and said that Frida was a surrealist. She did not agree. This phrase is attributed to her: they thought I was a surrealist, but I was not; I never painted dreams, I painted my own reality. The ex-votos on this staircase help explain why. Her way of telling stories came from the Mexican popular tradition, not from the dreams of Europe. /  / Before going up to the studio, notice what the staircase is like. It is made of stone, and on the walls there are portraits by José María Estrada, a Mexican painter of the nineteenth century who made portraits on commission. They are also part of the popular tradition that Frida admired. So the staircase works as a small gallery of folk art. /  / The Virgin of the Rosary of Talpa is a much-venerated image in Talpa de Allende, in Jalisco, where pilgrims arrive from many parts of Mexico. Many ex-votos are painted to thank her for a favor. That is why it is so common to find her image among them. /  / Now look at one of the paintings slowly. Read the text below, look for the saint at the top and try to imagine the story it tells. Then go up. Above, the room awaits where Frida painted many of her own miracles.

## Pieza: fri_08_estudio_caballete

### titulo

**ES:** El estudio de Frida

**EN:** Frida’s Studio

### frase_gancho

**ES:** Un caballete regalado por Nelson Rockefeller, pensado para pintar desde una silla de ruedas.

**EN:** An easel given by Nelson Rockefeller, designed for painting from a wheelchair.

### puente_narrativo

**ES:** Pasa del estudio a la recámara de día, donde hay una cama con un espejo encima.

**EN:** Move from the studio to the daytime bedroom, where there is a bed with a mirror above it.

### guion_corto

**ES:** El estudio de Frida es un cuarto luminoso y ordenado. Lo construyó el arquitecto Juan O'Gorman en mil novecientos cuarenta y seis, por encargo de Diego Rivera, y no formaba parte de la casa original. Aquí está su caballete, un regalo del empresario Nelson Rockefeller, con mecanismos para subir y bajar el lienzo cuando Frida pintaba desde su silla de ruedas. También están sus pinceles, sus pinturas y sus libros de poesía, arte, geografía, biología y medicina. Si miras bien, descubrirás frascos de barniz y de perfume que ella reutilizaba como recipientes de pintura. Es un espacio hecho para trabajar. Es un cuarto donde se trabajó de verdad.

**EN:** Frida’s studio is a bright, orderly room. The architect Juan O’Gorman built it in nineteen forty-six, commissioned by Diego Rivera, and it was not part of the original house. Here is her easel, a gift from the businessman Nelson Rockefeller, with mechanisms to raise and lower the canvas when Frida painted from her wheelchair. Her brushes, her paints, and her books of poetry, art, geography, biology, and medicine are here too. If you look closely, you will discover jars of varnish and perfume that she reused as containers for paint. It is a space made for work. It is a room where real work was done.
