### guion_largo

**ES:** En mil setecientos noventa, esta plaza se estaba empedrando y se hacían obras de agua. Ese año, la tierra devolvió dos piedras enormes que llevaban casi tres siglos enterradas. /  / La primera fue la Coatlicue. Apareció el trece de agosto, al excavar una zanja para un canal, cerca de lo que hoy es el Palacio Nacional. Es una escultura de andesita, tallada en bulto, con unos dos metros y medio de alto. **EN:** In seventeen ninety, this plaza was being paved and water works were under way. That year, the earth gave back two enormous stones that had lain buried for almost three centuries. /  / The first was the Coatlicue. It appeared on August thirteenth, when a trench was being dug for a canal, near what is now the National Palace. It is an andesite sculpture, carved in the round, about two and a half meters tall. It is symmetrical front and back, and every part is covered in reliefs. It is identified with the goddess Coatlicue, whose name means she of the skirt of serpents. Its identification is still debated, and some scholars have proposed that it represents other figures, such as the stars known as the tzitzimime. /  / The men of the time did not know what to do with it. It was taken to the university to be preserved and studied. But some clergymen feared that the Indigenous people would begin to venerate it again, and they ordered it buried once more in a courtyard. It was dug up in eighteen oh three so that Alexander von Humboldt could draw it, and again in eighteen twenty-three, to make a mold. It remained there until the first National Museum was founded. /  / The second stone came to light on December seventeenth, seventeen ninety, near the first. It is the Sun Stone, also called the Aztec Calendar. It is an enormous basalt sculpture. At the center is a face surrounded by the signs of the twenty days of the two-hundred-and-sixty-day calendar. For almost a century it was believed to be a calendar, because that is how Antonio de León y Gama interpreted it, having studied it right away. Today it is thought to have been a sacrificial altar, a cuauhxicalli, which means vessel of the eagles. It is said that Moctezuma the Second dedicated it a few years before the conquest. /  / Here is the detail that connects with the Cathedral. After it appeared, the Sun Stone was mounted on one of the towers of this church. It stayed set into the wall of the bell tower until eighteen eighty-five, when it was moved to the National Museum. A catalog from eighteen eighty-two still places it next to one of the towers. In other words, for almost a century people walked through this plaza and saw, attached to the Cathedral, one of the most representative works of Mexica culture. There was no clear explanation of what it was. /  / These discoveries hold a special place in the history of Mexican archaeology. They were the first signs that the city of Tenochtitlan was still down there. The Templo Mayor Museum, in fact, tells the story of the research starting in seventeen ninety. In this way the plaza, the Cathedral and the archaeological zone are linked. /  / Today, both the Coatlicue and the Sun Stone are in the National Museum of Anthropology, in Chapultepec. They are not on this tour, but they are well worth seeing there. /  / While you are here, imagine the scene. A trench, a group of workers, a blow of the pickax and suddenly a face of stone. The city that seemed forgotten reappeared without warning, and the authorities of the day did not know whether to display it, study it or hide it. That doubt has accompanied these discoveries to this day.

Along it you move toward the choir, the organs, and the main altar. Walk slowly and look at the floor, because it is not entirely level. **EN:** You have just entered one of the largest churches in the Americas. Before you look for altars and chapels, it is worth understanding how this space is built. /  / The Cathedral has five naves and a transept, that is, the transverse space that crosses the church. The interior adds up to fifty-one vaults, seventy-four arches, forty columns, and around one hundred fifty windows. These are round figures, which help you imagine the scale. What you can see at a glance is that the central nave is the widest and the tallest, and that the side naves accompany it like lower corridors. /  / This arrangement has its own logic. The central nave leads from the entrance to the altar, and along the way you find the elements that organize the temple. First comes the choir, with its wooden stalls and the two great organs. Then, the Altar del Perdón and the transept. At the far end, in the apse, stands the Capilla Mayor with the Altar de los Reyes. The side naves give access to the chapels, each one dedicated to a saint and sponsored by a religious order, a guild, or a confraternity. /  / Building this space took decades. The vaults were closed one by one beginning in fifteen eighty-one, and the last one was closed on the twenty-second of June, sixteen sixty-seven. That year is considered the end of the work on the interior. It was then that the temple was dedicated. You can see the result in the sobriety of its lines. The original design has a Renaissance air, and its order contrasts with the Baroque altars that were added later. /  / Now look up and trace the vaults with your eyes. Remember that the temple has some one hundred fifty windows, and that the light falling on the nave depends on them. /  / Then lower your gaze to the floor and walk with care. The floor of the Cathedral is not perfectly horizontal. This church stands on the soft ground of what was once a lake, and since its construction it has been sinking unevenly. At one point in the building the maximum unevenness reached more than two meters, and it was reduced by a later intervention. Further on, at another stop, you will see how it was straightened. For now, it is enough to notice that the floor is a kind of map of the ground. /  / One last observation. Despite its size, the space does not feel empty. It is filled by the presence of the altars, the iron screens, the pews of the faithful, and the side chapels, and everything keeps an order. If you happen to be here during a Mass, the atmosphere changes completely. You hear the organ, or singing, and the space fills with a meaning that cannot be appreciated when it is silent. Respect that ritual: speak in a low voice, avoid the flash, and do not disturb those who are praying. /  / When you are ready, continue along the nave toward the center of the temple.

## Pieza: tmc_08_coro_organos

### titulo

**ES:** El coro, la sillería y los dos órganos

**EN:** The Choir, the Choir Stalls and the Two Organs

### frase_gancho

**ES:** Un templo dentro del templo, con sillería tallada y dos órganos monumentales.

**EN:** A temple within the temple, with carved choir stalls and two monumental organs.

### puente_narrativo

**ES:** Sigue hacia el lado del Altar del Perdón, en la parte trasera del coro.

**EN:** Continue toward the side of the Altar of Forgiveness, at the back of the choir.

### guion_corto

**ES:** En el centro de la nave central hay otro espacio, rodeado de rejas y de madera tallada: el coro. Allí se sentaban los canónigos para rezar y cantar. La sillería es obra de Juan de Rojas, de finales del siglo diecisiete, y sus asientos tienen relieves tallados. Arriba de ellos están los dos grandes órganos del periodo virreinal, cuyas fachadas miden alrededor de nueve metros de ancho por once de alto. En esta Catedral fue coronado Agustín de Iturbide como emperador, el veintiuno de julio de mil ochocientos veintidós, en la capilla mayor, junto al altar. **EN:** In the center of the central nave there is another space, surrounded by iron screens and carved wood: the choir. Here the canons sat to pray and sing. The choir stalls are the work of Juan de Rojas, from the late seventeenth century, and their seats have carved reliefs. Above them are the two great organs of the viceregal period, whose facades measure about nine meters wide by eleven high. In this Cathedral, Agustín de Iturbide was crowned emperor on the twenty-first of July, eighteen twenty-two, in the main chapel, next to the altar. In nineteen sixty-seven a fire damaged most of these choir stalls and affected the organs, and restorations followed.

### guion_largo

**ES:** Estás en el centro del templo, donde se levanta uno de los espacios más especiales de una catedral antigua: el coro. En las catedrales de la Nueva España, el coro estaba en medio de la nave, cerrado por rejas, y allí se reunían los canónigos a rezar y a cantar el oficio varias veces al día. /  / La parte más llamativa es la sillería, el conjunto de asientos de madera tallada. La hizo Juan de Rojas entre mil seiscientos noventa y seis y mil seiscientos noventa y siete, con una madera llamada tapincerán, y tiene cincuenta y nueve relieves. Cada asiento mostraba escenas y figuras, y se usaba según el rango de quien lo ocupaba. Es un tipo de obra que reúne escultura, carpintería y liturgia en el mismo mueble. /  / Sobre la sillería se alzan los dos órganos. Son enormes: sus fachadas miden alrededor de nueve metros de ancho por once de alto, según el INAH, y se construyeron en el siglo dieciocho. Los actuales se atribuyen a José Nassarre, y se fechan entre mil setecientos treinta y cuatro y mil setecientos treinta y seis. Antes hubo un órgano anterior, de finales del siglo diecisiete. Con estos instrumentos sonaba el culto de la catedral más importante del virreinato. /  / En esta Catedral ocurrió también un momento político. Agustín de Iturbide fue coronado emperador de México el veintiuno de julio de mil ochocientos veintidós. La ceremonia no fue en el coro, sino en la capilla mayor, junto al altar, donde se colocaron los tronos. Venía de consumar la independencia el año anterior. Iturbide gobernó pocos meses y abdicó en mil ochocientos veintitrés. Sus restos descansan en otra capilla de la Catedral. /  / El diecisiete de enero de mil novecientos sesenta y siete, un cortocircuito provocó un incendio que empezó en el Altar del Perdón, justo detrás del coro. El fuego dañó la mayor parte de la sillería, pues se perdieron setenta y cinco de sus noventa y nueve asientos. También afectó los órganos. Después hubo trabajos de restauración, con intervenciones en mil novecientos setenta y seis y entre dos mil siete y dos mil ocho. /  / La reja que cierra el coro también tiene historia. Se hizo en Macao, en el sur de China, en mil setecientos veintidós, y se colocó en la Catedral en mil setecientos treinta. Muestra que esta catedral tuvo piezas hechas muy lejos. /  / El coro suele pedir una donación para entrar, y a veces está cerrado por celebraciones. Si no puedes entrar, también se aprecia mucho desde la nave, a través de las rejas, y los órganos se ven bien desde cualquier punto del crucero. /  / Piensa en cuánto sonido ha pasado por este lugar. Durante siglos, el coro fue la voz de la Catedral. Detrás de él, hacia el altar, hay una pieza que también sufrió el incendio y que tiene un nombre muy sugerente: el Altar del Perdón.

**EN:** You are standing in the center of the temple, where one of the most special spaces of an old cathedral rises: the choir. In the cathedrals of New Spain, the choir stood in the middle of the nave, closed off by iron screens, and the canons gathered there to pray and to sing the divine office several times a day. /  / The most striking part is the choir stalls, the set of carved wooden seats. Juan de Rojas made them between sixteen ninety-six and sixteen ninety-seven, using a wood called tapincerán, and they have fifty-nine reliefs. Each seat showed scenes and figures, and was used according to the rank of the person who occupied it. It is the kind of work that brings together sculpture, carpentry and liturgy in a single piece of furniture. /  / Above the choir stalls rise the two organs. They are enormous: their facades measure about nine meters wide by eleven high, according to the INAH, and they were built in the eighteenth century. The current ones are attributed to José Nassarre, and are dated between seventeen thirty-four and seventeen thirty-six. Before them there was an earlier organ, from the late seventeenth century. With these instruments, the worship of the most important cathedral in the viceroyalty came to life in sound. /  / A political moment also took place in this Cathedral. Agustín de Iturbide was crowned emperor of Mexico on the twenty-first of July, eighteen twenty-two. The ceremony was not held in the choir, but in the main chapel, next to the altar, where the thrones were placed. He had just completed independence the year before. Iturbide ruled for only a few months and abdicated in eighteen twenty-three. His remains rest in another chapel of the Cathedral. /  / On the seventeenth of January, nineteen sixty-seven, a short circuit caused a fire that began in the Altar of Forgiveness, right behind the choir. The fire damaged most of the choir stalls, as seventy-five of their ninety-nine seats were lost. It also affected the organs. Restoration work followed, with interventions in nineteen seventy-six and between two thousand seven and two thousand eight. /  / The iron screen that encloses the choir also has a history. It was made in Macao, in southern China, in seventeen twenty-two, and it was installed in the Cathedral in seventeen thirty. It shows that this cathedral held pieces made very far away. /  / The choir usually asks for a donation to enter, and sometimes it is closed for celebrations. If you cannot go in, it can also be appreciated very well from the nave, through the screens, and the organs can be seen clearly from any point of the crossing. /  / Think of how much sound has passed through this place. For centuries, the choir was the voice of the Cathedral. Behind it, toward the altar, there is a piece that also suffered in the fire and that has a very evocative name: the Altar of Forgiveness.

## Pieza: tmc_14_suelo_hundimiento

### titulo

**ES:** El suelo que se hunde: la corrección de la Catedral

**EN:** The Sinking Ground: Correcting the Cathedral

### frase_gancho

**ES:** Cuatro mil doscientos veinte metros cúbicos de arcilla retirados para enderezar un edificio de piedra.

**EN:** Four thousand two hundred twenty cubic meters of clay removed to straighten a stone building.

### puente_narrativo

**ES:** Sal de la Catedral con calma. Si sigues hacia el Templo Mayor, hazlo por el costado oriente, hacia la calle Seminario, donde empieza la zona arqueológica.

**EN:** Leave the Cathedral unhurriedly. If you continue toward the Templo Mayor, go along the east side, toward Seminario Street, where the archaeological zone begins.

### guion_corto

**ES:** Esta Catedral se hunde desde que se construyó, y se hunde de forma despareja. Está sobre el terreno blando de un antiguo lago. Hacia mil novecientos ochenta y nueve el deterioro era alarmante, y los ingenieros eligieron una técnica llamada subexcavación controlada. Consiste en retirar poco a poco arcilla de capas profundas, entre dieciocho y veinte metros bajo tierra, para que el suelo baje debajo de las zonas más altas y el edificio se endereze. Los trabajos comenzaron en mil novecientos noventa y tres y sacaron cerca de cuatro mil doscientos veinte metros cúbicos de arcilla. La meta no era volver a la verticalidad perfecta, sino recuperar más o menos la mitad de la deformación.

**EN:** This Cathedral has been sinking since it was built, and it sinks unevenly. It stands on the soft ground of an ancient lake. Around nineteen eighty-nine the damage was alarming, and engineers chose a technique called controlled subexcavation. It consists of gradually removing clay from deep layers, between eighteen and twenty meters underground, so that the ground drops beneath the higher areas and the building straightens. The work began in nineteen ninety-three and removed about four thousand two hundred twenty cubic meters of clay. The goal was not to return to perfect verticality, but to recover roughly half of the deformation.

### guion_largo

**ES:** Antes de dejar la Catedral, conviene entender el problema que más la ha preocupado en toda su historia: el suelo. Este edificio de piedra, pesado y enorme, está sobre un terreno que no es firme. /  / La Catedral se levantó en el rincón de la antigua isla de Tenochtitlan, sobre el fondo blando y húmedo de un lago que fue relleno. Debajo hay capas de arcilla que se comprimen con el peso y con el paso del tiempo. Además, el subsuelo no es parejo: bajo ciertas zonas hay restos prehispánicos, cimientos antiguos y rellenos distintos. Por eso la Catedral no se hunde de manera uniforme. Unas partes bajan más que otras, y eso produce inclinaciones y grietas. /  / El problema se notó desde la construcción. Ya en mil quinientos sesenta y dos, la cimentación comenzó a hundirse, y se rehízo con un pedraplén sobre estacas, que se dio por concluido en mil seiscientos quince. Durante los siglos siguientes se reforzó varias veces. Se calcula que el edificio, con sus cimientos, pesa ciento veintisiete mil toneladas. /  / Hacia mil novecientos ochenta y nueve el deterioro se había vuelto alarmante. Los expertos buscaron una solución que no dañara el monumento. Eligieron una técnica llamada subexcavación controlada. Funciona al revés de lo que se esperaría: no se refuerza el suelo, sino que se le quita un poco de arcilla. Se hacen pozos profundos, llamados lumbreras, y desde ellos se extraen pequeñas cantidades de arcilla en capas que están entre dieciocho y veinte metros bajo tierra. Al retirarla, el terreno baja un poco bajo las zonas más altas del edificio, y el conjunto se endereza. /  / Los trabajos comenzaron en mil novecientos noventa y tres. La etapa principal fue de mil novecientos noventa y tres a mil novecientos noventa y ocho, con treinta y dos lumbreras de veinte metros, y los trabajos se prolongaron hasta principios de dos mil tres. En total se retiraron cerca de cuatro mil doscientos veinte metros cúbicos de arcilla. /  / La meta no era volver a un edificio perfectamente vertical. El objetivo era recuperar más o menos la mitad de la deformación acumulada. El desnivel máximo era de más de dos metros y se redujo en poco más de ochenta centímetros, alrededor de una tercera parte. Es un resultado modesto en números, pero importante en estabilidad. /  / Gracias a ese trabajo, la Catedral salió de la lista de monumentos en mayor riesgo del World Monuments Fund, en el año dos mil. Eso no significa que el problema desapareció. Un estudio de la UNAM advierte que el hundimiento no se ha detenido, y que el conjunto se vigila con instrumentos que miden cada movimiento. /  / Los pisos interiores de la Catedral son irregulares. No es un defecto de la obra, sino la huella de una ciudad que sigue asentándose sobre un lago. /  / Hay una ironía en todo esto. Los mexicas también construyeron sobre un lago. El Templo Mayor se levantó por etapas, cada una encima de la anterior, y sus ruinas están a pocos pasos de aquí.

**EN:** Before leaving the Cathedral, it is worth understanding the problem that has worried it most throughout its history: the ground. This heavy, enormous stone building stands on terrain that is not firm. /  / The Cathedral was raised in the corner of the ancient island of Tenochtitlan, on the soft, damp bed of a lake that was filled in. Beneath it lie layers of clay that compress under the weight and with the passing of time. Moreover, the subsoil is not even: under certain areas there are pre-Hispanic remains, ancient foundations, and different kinds of fill. That is why the Cathedral does not sink uniformly. Some parts go down more than others, and this produces tilting and cracks. /  / The problem was noticed during construction. As early as 1562, the foundation began to sink, and it was rebuilt with a rubble embankment on piles, which was declared complete in 1615. Over the following centuries it was reinforced several times. The building, together with its foundations, is estimated to weigh one hundred twenty-seven thousand tons. /  / Around nineteen eighty-nine the deterioration had become alarming. Experts looked for a solution that would not damage the monument. They chose a technique called controlled subexcavation. It works the opposite way from what one would expect: the ground is not reinforced; instead, a little clay is taken away. Deep shafts, called lumbreras, are made, and from them small amounts of clay are extracted from layers lying between eighteen and twenty meters underground. As the clay is removed, the ground drops slightly beneath the highest parts of the building, and the whole structure straightens. /  / The work began in nineteen ninety-three. The main stage ran from nineteen ninety-three to nineteen ninety-eight, with thirty-two shafts twenty meters deep, and the work continued until early two thousand three. In total, about four thousand two hundred twenty cubic meters of clay were removed. /  / The goal was not to return to a perfectly vertical building. The objective was to recover roughly half of the accumulated deformation. The maximum difference in level was more than two meters, and it was reduced by a little over eighty centimeters, about a third. It is a modest result in numbers, but an important one for stability. /  / Thanks to that work, the Cathedral came off the World Monuments Fund list of the monuments at greatest risk, in the year two thousand. That does not mean the problem has disappeared. A UNAM study warns that the sinking has not stopped, and that the complex is monitored with instruments that measure every movement. /  / The interior floors of the Cathedral are uneven. It is not a flaw in the construction, but the mark of a city that keeps settling on a lake. /  / There is an irony in all this. The Mexica also built on a lake. The Templo Mayor was raised in stages, each one on top of the previous one, and its ruins are just a few steps from here.
