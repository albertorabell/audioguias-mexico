Lleva círculos con forma de cascabel y los labios curvados hacia abajo, en un gesto de muerte. Según el mito de Coatepec, Huitzilopochtli la decapitó y arrojó su cuerpo cerro abajo. Lo más curioso está en la parte inferior, donde termina el cuello. Allí, fuera de la vista, el escultor grabó el signo del agua y el fuego, junto con serpientes. La abadesa del convento prefirió donarla al Museo Nacional antes que venderla a coleccionistas extranjeros.

**EN:** In March 1830, workers were building houses for the Convent of la Concepción, in the Historic Center of Mexico City. Then a stone head with drooping eyelids came to light. ### puente_narrativo

**EN:** Where does Tlaltecuhtli usually hide? Almost always below. Mexica sculptors carved her in relief on the underside of other works, pressed against the ground, as happens on the base of the Coatlicue. This sculpture is different: it is a freestanding figure, worked on all sides. Tlaltecuhtli is the Mexica deity of the earth. Some call her the Coatlicue of the Metro, but she is not Coatlicue. Look at the posture: she is squatting, with her legs bent as in childbirth, and claws extend from her hands and feet. The face is fleshless and the mouth opens, ready to swallow. On her chest she wears a necklace of hearts and hands. For the Mexica, the earth was a living being that gave food and received the dead. This sculpture puts that idea right in front of you.

**EN:** Tlaltecuhtli is the Mexica deity of the earth, and she is almost always seen hidden away. Sculptors carved her in relief on the underside of other works, as happens on the base of the Coatlicue. That way she stayed pressed against the ground. This sculpture breaks with that custom. It is a freestanding figure, worked on all sides and made to be seen from the front. /  / Some call her the Coatlicue of the Metro, but she is not Coatlicue. With one half they made the sky and with the other the earth. /  / From the deity’s body sprang trees, flowers, springs and caves. But it is said that the earth wept at night and asked for blood and hearts in order to bear its fruits. /  / That is why this figure does not smile. She is the earth that nourishes and the earth that demands, brought together in a single image.

And in July 1926 it arrived at the National Museum, where it is kept today as one of the most famous images of the Mexica world.

### puente_narrativo

### guion_corto

**ES:** Chalchiuhtlicue significa la de la falda de jade. Es el nombre en náhuatl de la diosa de las aguas terrestres, y esta escultura teotihuacana la representa con más de tres metros de alto. **EN:** Chalchiuhtlicue means the one with the jade skirt. It is the Nahuatl name of the goddess of terrestrial waters, and this Teotihuacana sculpture portrays her at more than three meters tall.
