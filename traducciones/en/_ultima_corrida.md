**EN:** Pause for a moment and look at the horizon. To the north, a volcanic mountain dominates the landscape: it is Cerro Gordo. Before you stretches a city that grew to have around one hundred thousand inhabitants in about twenty square kilometers, the largest in Mesoamerica. Notice the walls around you: almost all of them combine a sloping base, the slope, with a rectangular frame on top, the panel. That design repeats for several kilometers along its central axis, the Avenue of the Dead. Its original name was lost; the Mexica gave it the name Teotihuacán centuries later, and it is usually translated as “the place where the gods were born.”

**EN:** Almost none of the names you will hear today belonged to the people who raised these stones. Teotihuacán is the name the Mexica gave it centuries later, when the city was already empty. The Mexica also christened the central avenue the Avenue of the Dead, because they believed the mounds along its sides were tombs of ancient kings. They were not: they are temples and palaces. In Maya inscriptions, the city appears under another name, Puh, which means Place of Rushes. What its own inhabitants called it remains a mystery. /  / Why did a city of this scale grow here? Because the valley had almost everything. There were springs, fertile land, and clay for building. Very close by were the obsidian mines of Pachuca, the volcanic glass from which the finest blades and arrowheads in Mesoamerica were made. That obsidian was sold as far as Monte Albán and Tikal, and the state oversaw its production and trade. Some researchers think that families displaced by the volcanic eruptions in the southern Basin of Mexico also arrived. The exact order of those events is still debated. /  / The city planners laid out the city as a giant grid. The Avenue of the Dead runs from south to north, and two great avenues cross it at right angles. But if you carried a compass, you would notice something strange: the entire city is turned about fifteen degrees east of true north. The central part follows the orientation of the Pyramid of the Sun. Its facade faces the place where the Sun sets at the end of April and in mid-August. Between those dates, two hundred sixty days are counted, the length of the ritual calendar. /  / The story you are about to walk through has four great moments. Between the first and second centuries, the pyramids of the Sun and the Moon are raised. Around the year two hundred, the Citadel is built with its great temple. From the fourth century onward comes the peak: it is estimated that there were some two thousand apartment compounds, each with dozens of people. There were also neighborhoods of artisans and people who came from Oaxaca, Veracruz, and the Maya region. /  / The fourth moment is the end. Around the year five hundred fifty, a fire devastated the elite buildings along the Avenue. There is no trace of a foreign invasion. Among the explanations mentioned are droughts, famine, and internal revolts. The city did not disappear overnight: it emptied little by little. Almost everything we know today has been reconstructed by archaeologists over more than a century, and open questions remain. /  / This guide moves from south to north, just like the Avenue. Some stops show you what is in plain view. Others work like X-rays: they tell you what lies hidden beneath your feet, such as tunnels and sealed offerings.

## Pieza: teo_01_ciudadela

### titulo

**ES:** La Ciudadela y la Gran Plaza

**EN:** The Ciudadela and the Great Plaza

### frase_gancho

**ES:** Los españoles creyeron que era una fortaleza. Era el corazón del poder.

**EN:** The Spaniards thought it was a fortress. It was the heart of power.

### puente_narrativo

**ES:** Cruza la gran explanada hacia el edificio del fondo oriental: detrás de la plataforma gris que lo cubre te espera la fachada más sagrada de la ciudad.

**EN:** Cross the great esplanade toward the building at the eastern end: behind the gray platform that covers it, the most sacred facade in the city awaits you.

### guion_corto

**ES:** Sube a la plataforma de entrada y asómate a esta explanada monumental. Cuando los españoles vieron este recinto cerrado por altos muros de cuatrocientos metros por lado, creyeron que era un cuartel militar y lo llamaron La Ciudadela. En realidad estás ante el corazón político y ceremonial de Teotihuacán. Mira hacia el patio hundido: es tan vasto que podía reunir a una multitud enorme. Alrededor, sobre las plataformas que te rodean, se levantan quince pequeñas pirámides, y al fondo domina el Templo de la Serpiente Emplumada.

**EN:** Climb up to the entrance platform and look out over this monumental esplanade. When the Spaniards saw this compound enclosed by high walls four hundred meters on each side, they thought it was a military barracks and called it the Ciudadela. In reality, you are standing before the political and ceremonial heart of Teotihuacán. Look toward the sunken courtyard: it is so vast that it could gather an enormous crowd. All around, on the platforms that surround you, fifteen small pyramids rise, and at the far end the Temple of the Feathered Serpent dominates.

### guion_largo

**ES:** La palabra Ciudadela la inventaron los españoles, que vieron altos muros y pensaron en un fuerte. Pero fíjate cómo está hecho este lugar. Son cuatro plataformas unidas que rodean una plaza inmensa, y tiene una sola entrada pública, en el lado poniente. Eso no sirve para defenderse de un ejército. Sirve para controlar quién entra y cuándo. Los arqueólogos piensan que el recinto pudo permanecer cerrado casi todos los días y abrirse solo para ceremonias especiales. /  / Alrededor de la plaza se alinean quince templos pequeños, incrustados en los bordes de las plataformas: cuatro al poniente, cuatro al norte, cuatro al sur y tres al oriente. Y al oriente, frente a quien entra, domina el Templo de la Serpiente Emplumada. Es el tercer edificio más grande de toda la ciudad, después de las pirámides del Sol y de la Luna. Todo el conjunto se construyó entre los años ciento cincuenta y doscientos cincuenta de nuestra era. Una obra así necesitó un Estado capaz de organizar a miles de trabajadores y de reunir materiales de todo el valle. /  / Imagina este patio en un día de fiesta. Se ha calculado que la plaza pudo recibir a decenas de miles de personas, aunque eso es una estimación de capacidad y no un dato comprobado. Desde abajo, las plataformas laterales te encierran: el paisaje del valle desaparece y toda la atención se dirige hacia los sacerdotes en lo alto de los basamentos y hacia el templo del fondo. Era un escenario diseñado para que cualquiera sintiera el peso del Estado y del cosmos. /  / Algunos investigadores proponen que la plaza se llegó a inundar de forma ritual, como un espejo de agua rodeado de templos. Las marcas de agua que aparecieron en el túnel que corre bajo el recinto lo sugieren. Es una hipótesis, pero encaja con la importancia del agua en toda la ciudad. /  / Bajo estas plataformas hay además construcciones más antiguas. Antes de levantar la Ciudadela, los constructores arrasaron una estructura anterior que se parece a una cancha de juego de pelota. Teotihuacán se rehízo a sí misma más de una vez, y aquí puedes verlo: cada plataforma esconde etapas anteriores que todavía se estudian. /  / Hacia el año trescientos cincuenta ocurrió un cambio profundo. La fachada del Templo de la Serpiente Emplumada quedó oculta detrás de una gran plataforma, la Plataforma Adosada, que subirás en la siguiente parada. Unos estudiosos lo leen como un cambio en el gobierno de la ciudad. Otros piensan que fue un entierro ritual hecho con respeto. Mucho después, en una fecha que no conocemos, alguien dañó las esculturas de los lados que quedaron descubiertos. /  / Estás a más de dos mil metros sobre el nivel del mar y casi siempre sin sombra. Antes de salir hacia la Calzada de los Muertos, toma unos sorbos de agua.

**EN:** The word Ciudadela was invented by the Spaniards, who saw high walls and thought of a fort. But notice how this place is built. It consists of four joined platforms surrounding an immense plaza, and it has only one public entrance, on the west side. That is no use for defending against an army. It serves to control who enters and when. Archaeologists think the compound may have stayed closed most days and opened only for special ceremonies. /  / Around the plaza stand fifteen small temples, set into the edges of the platforms: four to the west, four to the north, four to the south, and three to the east. And to the east, facing whoever enters, the Temple of the Feathered Serpent dominates. It is the third largest building in the entire city, after the Pyramids of the Sun and the Moon. The whole complex was built between the years one hundred fifty and two hundred fifty of our era. A project like this required a state capable of organizing thousands of workers and of gathering materials from all over the valley. /  / Imagine this courtyard on a festival day. It has been calculated that the plaza could hold tens of thousands of people, although that is an estimate of capacity and not a proven fact. From below, the side platforms enclose you: the landscape of the valley disappears, and all attention turns toward the priests high on the platforms and toward the temple at the far end. It was a stage designed so that anyone would feel the weight of the state and of the cosmos. /  / Some researchers propose that the plaza was at times flooded ritually, like a mirror of water surrounded by temples. The water marks found in the tunnel that runs beneath the compound suggest this. It is a hypothesis, but it fits with the importance of water throughout the city. /  / Beneath these platforms there are also older constructions. Before raising the Ciudadela, the builders leveled an earlier structure that resembles a ball court. Teotihuacán remade itself more than once, and here you can see it: each platform hides earlier phases that are still being studied. /  / Around the year three hundred fifty, a profound change took place. The facade of the Temple of the Feathered Serpent was hidden behind a large platform, the Adosada Platform, which you will climb at the next stop. Some scholars read this as a change in the government of the city. Others think it was a ritual burial carried out with respect. Much later, at a date we do not know, someone damaged the sculptures on the sides that remained exposed. /  / You are more than two thousand meters above sea level and almost always without shade. Before leaving toward the Avenue of the Dead, take a few sips of water.

## Pieza: teo_02_templo_serpiente

### titulo

**ES:** Templo de la Serpiente Emplumada

**EN:** Temple of the Feathered Serpent

### frase_gancho

**ES:** Más de ciento treinta personas fueron enterradas bajo sus cimientos para consagrar este templo.

**EN:** More than one hundred thirty people were buried beneath its foundations to consecrate this temple.

### puente_narrativo

**ES:** Si sigues la ruta completa, quédate frente a esta escalinata para asomarte con rayos X al túnel sellado bajo tus pies; si vas en la ruta exprés, sal de la Ciudadela hacia la Calzada de los Muertos y camina rumbo al norte.

**EN:** If you are following the full route, stay in front of this staircase to peer with X-rays into the sealed tunnel beneath your feet; if you are on the express route, leave the Ciudadela for the Avenue of the Dead and walk north.

### guion_corto

**ES:** Cuando te acercas de frente a este edificio, lo primero que ves es una estructura lisa y gris llamada Plataforma Adosada. Sube sus escalones y asómate al pasillo estrecho que se abre detrás: ahí aparece, oculta durante siglos, la fachada esculpida más espectacular de Teotihuacán. Estás frente al Templo de la Serpiente Emplumada, levantado hacia el año doscientos. De sus tableros sobresalen enormes cabezas de piedra que antes estuvieron pintadas de rojo, verde, azul y amarillo. Unas son serpientes emplumadas que emergen de una flor; las otras, con dos anillos en la frente y sin mandíbula inferior, representan, según muchos especialistas, un tocado sagrado del origen del tiempo.

**EN:** As you approach this building head-on, the first thing you see is a smooth gray structure called the Adosada Platform. Climb its steps and look into the narrow passage that opens behind it: there appears, hidden for centuries, the most spectacular sculpted facade in Teotihuacán. You are standing before the Temple of the Feathered Serpent, built around the year 200. Enormous stone heads project from its panels, which were once painted red, green, blue and yellow. Some are feathered serpents emerging from a flower; the others, with two rings on the forehead and no lower jaw, represent, according to many specialists, a sacred headdress from the origin of time.

### guion_largo

**ES:** Esta fachada se hizo para leerse. Dos criaturas se alternan a lo largo de los tableros y de la escalinata. La primera es la Serpiente Emplumada. Su cabeza de bulto brota de una corola de pétalos y está rodeada de caracoles y conchas, como si nadara en las aguas primordiales de la creación. La segunda desconcertó a los arqueólogos durante décadas. Por los dos anillos que lleva en la frente se pensó que era Tláloc, el dios de la lluvia. Hoy muchos especialistas la interpretan como el tocado de Cipactli, el cocodrilo monstruoso de la Tierra y símbolo del primer día del calendario. Según esta lectura, la serpiente carga sobre el lomo el comienzo del tiempo. /  / Se calcula que entre trescientas y cuatrocientas de estas cabezas, de cerca de un metro de alto, decoraban el edificio. Y todas estuvieron pintadas. Imagina esta pared completa, brillante, vista desde la plaza llena de gente. /  / Las fechas no son exactas: los estudios sitúan la obra entre los años ciento cincuenta y doscientos cincuenta. Pero lo más impactante estaba bajo el piso. Antes de levantar el templo, hacia el año doscientos, enterraron aquí a decenas de personas. Los arqueólogos Rubén Cabrera y Saburo Sugiyama encontraron los restos de ciento treinta y siete, colocados con gran orden y con las manos atadas a la espalda. Si se suman las zonas que no se han excavado, se calcula que fueron entre doscientas y doscientas sesenta. /  / ¿Quiénes eran? Los análisis del oxígeno en sus huesos y dientes dieron una sorpresa. Venían de varias regiones de Mesoamérica, pero la mayoría había vivido en Teotihuacán durante años. No eran simples cautivos recién llegados. Una hipótesis propone que eran extranjeros al servicio del gobernante, tal vez guardias de élite, porque llevaban atuendos y ofrendas muy parecidos y muy ricos. Es una hipótesis, no una certeza. Otras ideas proponen que eran sirvientes de un fundador de dinastía, o que estaban dedicados a la Serpiente Emplumada o al propio edificio. Junto con ellos se enterró un tesoro: se habla de alrededor de cien mil objetos preciosos. /  / Una cosa más sobre la historia de este edificio. Unos cien años después de su construcción, hacia el año trescientos cincuenta, la fachada quedó tapada por la Plataforma Adosada que acabas de subir. Se discute por qué. Una lectura habla de un cambio en el gobierno interno de la ciudad; otra, de un entierro ritual respetuoso. Los lados que no se cubrieron fueron dañados en una fecha desconocida, y por eso la parte oculta es la que mejor se conservó. /  / Entre mil novecientos diecisiete y mil novecientos veintidós, el arqueólogo Manuel Gamio abrió el pasillo en el que estás parado. Devolvió a la luz una fachada que nadie había visto en más de mil quinientos años.

**EN:** This facade was made to be read. Two creatures alternate along the panels and the staircase. The first is the Feathered Serpent. Its sculpted head bursts from a corolla of petals and is surrounded by snails and shells, as if it were swimming in the primordial waters of creation. The second puzzled archaeologists for decades. Because of the two rings on its forehead, it was thought to be Tláloc, the god of rain. Today many specialists interpret it as the headdress of Cipactli, the monstrous crocodile of the Earth and symbol of the first day of the calendar. According to this reading, the serpent carries the beginning of time on its back. /  / It is estimated that between three hundred and four hundred of these heads, about one meter high, decorated the building. And all of them were painted. Imagine this entire wall, brilliant, seen from a plaza full of people. /  / The dates are not exact: studies place the construction between the years 150 and 250. But the most striking thing lay beneath the floor. Before the temple was raised, around the year 200, dozens of people were buried here. The archaeologists Rubén Cabrera and Saburo Sugiyama found the remains of one hundred thirty-seven, placed in great order and with their hands tied behind their backs. If the areas that have not been excavated are added, it is estimated that there were between two hundred and two hundred sixty. /  / Who were they? Analyses of the oxygen in their bones and teeth produced a surprise. They came from several regions of Mesoamerica, but most had lived in Teotihuacán for years. They were not simply captives who had just arrived. One hypothesis proposes that they were foreigners in the service of the ruler, perhaps elite guards, because they wore very similar and very rich attire and offerings. It is a hypothesis, not a certainty. Other ideas propose that they were servants of a dynasty founder, or that they were dedicated to the Feathered Serpent or to the building itself. A treasure was buried alongside them: there is talk of around one hundred thousand precious objects. /  / One more thing about the history of this building. About one hundred years after its construction, around the year 350, the facade was covered by the Adosada Platform that you have just climbed. Why is debated. One reading speaks of a change in the city's internal government; another, of a respectful ritual burial. The sides that were not covered were damaged at an unknown date, and that is why the hidden part is the best preserved. /  / Between nineteen seventeen and nineteen twenty-two, the archaeologist Manuel Gamio opened the passage where you are standing. He brought back into the light a facade that no one had seen in more than one thousand five hundred years.

## Pieza: teo_03_tunel_tlalocan

### titulo

It is the Attached Platform, made up of three small platforms joined to the west facade. It was built after the pyramid, in at least four stages, and it was decorated with heavy sculptures of felines, feathered serpents and fantastic beings, as well as mural painting. By comparison with the murals of Atetelco and Tetitla, it is thought that they showed felines in procession. /  / The builders also hid offerings inside the building, along an east-to-west axis, close to the natural rock. There, archaeologists found obsidian points, vessels with the face of Tláloc, seashells, slate disks, a green stone mask, and the remains of an eagle, snakes, pumas, wolves and a condor. In the fill, a child between four and six years old also appeared, with a head deliberately deformed. Those who study these offerings see them as a way of consecrating the building as a sacred mountain. It would also have been a way of proclaiming the political and religious power of the city. /  / This pyramid also marks time. Its facade faces the point where the Sun sets in late April and mid-August. Between the August sunset and the following April one, two hundred sixty days go by, the count of the ritual calendar. The sunrise along that same orientation, in mid-February and late October, is also separated by two hundred sixty days. /  / Archaeologists have also made their way through the inside of this pyramid. Between nineteen seventeen and nineteen eighteen, Manuel Gamio opened a tunnel ninety-seven meters long toward the center. In nineteen thirty-three, another one of one hundred sixteen meters was opened. This made it possible to understand how it was put together. /  / What you see today is not exactly what the Teotihuacán people saw. Between nineteen oh five and nineteen oh seven, Leopoldo Batres excavated it, consolidated it and partly rebuilt it, for the celebrations of the Centennial of Independence. Since nineteen ten, it has been part of the first archaeological zone open to the public in Mexico. And beneath its foundations lies a cave that was discovered by chance in nineteen seventy-one, which we will talk about if you follow the full route. /  / It is sunny, and you are more than two thousand meters above sea level. Drink water before moving on.

They discovered that inside there are six earlier pyramids, one covering the other, until reaching the seventh, the one you see today. From the fourth building onward, each enlargement was consecrated with burials and offerings. And the enlargements were not small: Building 4 was nine times larger than Building 3. /  / The first of these discoveries appeared when Building 4 was under construction, in the first half of the third century. It was a man between forty and fifty years old, seated, with his hands tied behind his back. Analysis of his bones indicates that he spent his childhood far from Teotihuacán.
